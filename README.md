# Populism in Donor Countries and Bilateral Development Aid

Replication code for my Master's thesis in Economics **Universite Paris-Saclay**.
Author: **Audrey Leticia KEMGNE MEKA** · Contact: **audrey.kemgne-meka@universite-paris-salay.fr**

## Research question

Does the rise of populist parties in donor countries change the volume and composition of their official development assistance (ODA)? The thesis estimates event-study (two-way fixed effects) models around the year in which a donor's populist vote share first crosses a threshold, and studies the effect on total aid, on aid by sector and on aid by recipient region. Callaway & Sant'Anna (2021) estimates and alternative thresholds are used as robustness checks.

### Sample

The final sample consists of 422 donor-year observations covering 28 donor countries over 2002-2021. Most donors are observed throughout the period; several Central and Eastern European donors enter only after joining the OECD-DAC or becoming covered by the populism dataset.

### Variable construction

- **Aggregation.** Transactions are summed at the donor-year level (commitments and disbursements).
- **Sectors.** Disbursements are grouped using OECD-DAC sector classifications into: social, governance, economic infrastructure, humanitarian, agriculture, environment, plus a residual category (general budget support, trade policy, administrative costs, etc.).
- **Recipient regions.** World Bank groups: Sub-Saharan Africa, Europe & Central Asia, Middle East & North Africa, South Asia, East Asia & Pacific, Latin America & Caribbean. A separate category captures aid to unspecified or regional recipients.
- **Financing channel.** Grants, loans, other official flows, private finance.
- **Outcomes.** `ln(1 + disbursements)`, in total and by sector, region and channel.
- **Treatment.** A donor is treated from the first year in which its populist vote share exceeds 50% (baseline), with thresholds of 40% and 60% in the robustness checks. Treatment is absorbing: donors that leave treatment after entering it are dropped. The event window is [-5, +5] with t = -1 as the reference period.

## How to run

1. Place the raw data in `data/raw/`.
2. Open `code/00_master.do` and edit the single line `global root` so that it points to this folder.
3. On the first run, set `local install_packages 1` in the master file to install the required packages.
4. Run `00_master.do`. The do-files are executed in numerical order. Each file can also be run alone once the globals are defined.

**Software:** Stata 16 or later. Required user-written packages: `reghdfe`, `ftools`, `coefplot`, `estout`, `csdid`, `drdid`.

## Notes

- Country names are harmonised across sources (e.g. Czech Republic -> Czechia, Slovak Republic -> Slovakia, Türkiye -> Turkey). Populist vote-share country codes are mapped to names in `01_clean.do`.
- Event-time coding: `event_time_shift = t + 5`, so shift 4 corresponds to t = -1 (reference) and shift 5 to t = 0.
- Random seed for the wild bootstrap in `csdid`: 12345.
